Case Study · AI + Business Systems · 2026

From market noise to product decisions.

Raydar is an AI-assisted commerce intelligence system I built to turn scattered trend signals, market evidence, and product ideas into structured, explainable decisions about what deserves production time.

Core product principleCode calculates. AI interprets. Humans decide.
01 / Problem

More information wasn’t the problem.

Fragmented signals

Running a digital product business means constantly watching trend movement, competitor activity, seasonal timing, product patterns, and prior performance across different sources.

Decision friction

The bottleneck was turning all of that information into a consistent answer to a simple operational question: what should be built next, and why?

02 / Solution

A decision system, not another dashboard.

Raydar structures the evidence, applies deterministic scoring, makes the reasoning inspectable, and moves owner-approved opportunities through a workflow from research to launch and performance review.

03 / Architecture

Deterministic where it should be.

Code calculates

Numerical scores are produced by explicit logic rather than an LLM. That keeps recommendations reproducible, inspectable, and easier to debug.

AI interprets

AI belongs at the interpretation layer: summarizing evidence, identifying patterns, and helping explain why an opportunity might deserve attention.

Humans decide

The system can organize, compare, score, and recommend. The owner still decides whether an opportunity moves forward, changes direction, or gets rejected.

Performance closes the loop

Once a product reaches market, performance data becomes new evidence so future decisions can improve instead of treating every launch as an isolated event.

04 / Scoring

Explainable opportunity scoring.

Demand 20%
Trend Momentum 15%
Repeatability 15%
Customization Potential 15%
Product Fit 15%
Competition Gap 10%
Confidence 10%
Saturation Risk −10%

Dimensions are scored from 0–100. Recommendation thresholds turn the weighted score into actions such as BUILD NOW, TEST, WATCH, HOLD, or DEPRIORITIZE.

05 / Product judgment

Private operations. Public proof.

Real workflow

The system was designed around an actual commerce operation and the practical decisions involved in choosing, producing, and evaluating digital products.

Portfolio-safe demo

The public build uses synthetic and anonymized data. It does not expose private competitor intelligence, real sales records, credentials, owner information, or paid AI endpoints.

06 / Engineering

Built to demonstrate the system, not fake production.

Stack

Next.js, TypeScript, React, Recharts, Lucide icons, CSS, Git/GitHub, and Vercel.

Public-demo boundary

The demo does not persist visitor actions or make paid live LLM calls. Synthetic metrics illustrate workflows without being presented as business results.

07 / Learning

The hard part is deciding where AI should stop.

Raydar reinforced the product principle I keep returning to: useful automation is not measured by how much human judgment it removes. The better system is often the one that improves the quality, speed, and consistency of a decision while keeping accountability with the person making it.
